## Project Overview

**BATA NEXT** is an independent Business Analytics case study exploring how Bata can strengthen consideration and relevance among younger consumers.

The project began with a primary consumer survey and developed into a complete analytics and strategy workflow involving:

- Primary consumer research
- Data cleaning and transformation in Microsoft Excel
- Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
- PivotTables and cross-analysis
- Power BI dashboard development
- DAX measures
- Consumer behaviour analysis
- Brand perception analysis
- Competitor benchmarking
- Gap Analysis
- As-Is vs To-Be Analysis
- Customer segmentation and positioning
- Strategic recommendations
- Implementation roadmap
- Business KPIs

The objective was not simply to create a dashboard, but to demonstrate how **data can be transformed into actionable business recommendations**.

---

# Business Problem

Bata is a highly familiar footwear brand with strong associations around affordability, quality and reliability.

However, the exploratory survey revealed an interesting pattern:

> **Strong familiarity and previous usage were not translating into current first-choice preference among surveyed young consumers.**

This led to the refined business question:

### How can Bata strengthen awareness, positioning and perceived relevance of its existing youth-oriented footwear portfolio while retaining its established affordability and quality associations?

---

# Research Objective

The project aimed to:

1. Understand footwear purchasing behaviour among younger consumers.
2. Analyse current footwear brand preferences.
3. Examine consumer perceptions of Bata.
4. Identify barriers affecting purchase consideration.
5. Understand what could increase interest in Bata.
6. Compare Bata with selected competitors.
7. Identify potential perception and positioning gaps.
8. Develop practical business recommendations and measurable KPIs.

---

# Key Survey Findings

## 1. Sneakers dominated footwear preference

**8 of 14 respondents** mainly purchased sneakers.

This indicates strong relevance of casual and youth-oriented footwear within the surveyed sample.

---

## 2. Respondents were relatively price-conscious

**10 of 14 respondents** usually spent below **₹2,000** on a pair of footwear.

This suggests accessible pricing may remain important when targeting younger consumers.

---

## 3. Bata had strong previous usage

**11 of 14 respondents** had purchased Bata previously.

However:

### 0 respondents selected Bata as their current first-choice footwear brand.

This created one of the central findings of the project:

> **A familiarity-to-preference gap.**

---

## 4. Purchase consideration was moderate

Average likelihood of considering Bata for the next footwear purchase:

### **3.0 / 5**

This suggests respondents were not strongly rejecting Bata, but the brand was also not generating very strong consideration.

---

## 5. Bata's strongest perceptions were functional

The most frequently selected Bata perceptions were:

- **Affordable — 5 respondents**
- **Good Quality — 5 respondents**

This suggests Bata retains strong functional value associations within the sample.

---

## 6. Style relevance emerged as an important barrier

**7 of 14 respondents** selected either:

- Not trendy enough
- Designs do not match my taste

This suggests that product styling and perceived trend relevance may influence consideration among younger respondents.

---

## 7. Sneakers represented the strongest opportunity

The most requested improvements were:

- **More Sneakers — 6 respondents**
- **Trendier Designs — 5 respondents**
- **Student-Friendly Pricing — 5 respondents**

---

# Core Business Insight

The exploratory research suggests that Bata retains strong familiarity, affordability and quality associations among respondents.

However, these strengths are not fully translating into current first-choice preference.

The findings indicate that the opportunity may lie in strengthening:

- Youth-facing product visibility
- Contemporary styling perception
- Sneaker positioning
- Digital communication
- Student-oriented value propositions

---

# Secondary Research

Secondary research revealed that Bata is **already investing in youth-oriented products and initiatives**, including:

- Sneakerisation
- Power and North Star footwear ranges
- Digital channels
- Influencer marketing
- Youth-focused campaigns
- Accessible price points

This significantly changed the initial business diagnosis.

The problem was therefore refined from:

> "Bata needs more youth-oriented products"

to:

> **"Bata may need to improve how its existing youth-oriented products are perceived, discovered and positioned."**

---

# Competitor Benchmarking

Bata was benchmarked against:

- Campus
- Red Tape
- PUMA

Key dimensions included:

- Sneaker portfolio
- Price positioning
- Discount visibility
- Youth relevance
- Product presentation
- Digital positioning
- Lifestyle communication

### Key Benchmarking Insight

Bata already competes effectively on affordability and product availability.

However:

- Campus and Red Tape compete aggressively on price and visible product variety.
- PUMA builds stronger lifestyle and trend-led brand associations.

This suggests the competitive gap may involve **perception and positioning rather than simple product availability**.

# Business Analysis

The project applied several Business Analysis frameworks.

## Gap Analysis

Key gaps identified included:

- Brand familiarity → Current preference
- Functional perception → Contemporary perception
- Sneaker availability → Sneaker awareness
- Youth initiatives → Consumer recognition
- Product availability → Product discoverability

---

## As-Is vs To-Be

| As-Is | To-Be |
|---|---|
| Known and previously purchased | Known and actively considered |
| Affordable and good quality | Affordable and stylish |
| Sneakers available | Sneakers visibly relevant |
| Functional brand cues | Functional + fashionable |
| Moderate consideration | Stronger purchase consideration |

---

# Target Consumer

## Primary Target

### The Value-Conscious Trend Seeker

**Typical characteristics:**

- Age: approximately 18–25
- Student or early-career professional
- Primarily spends below ₹2,000
- Prefers sneakers and casual footwear
- Values style, comfort and affordability
- Wants contemporary products without premium pricing

### Proposed Positioning Direction

> **Affordable, reliable and contemporary footwear for young consumers who want everyday comfort without compromising on style.**

Conceptual project positioning line:

> **"Style that fits your life and your budget."**

---

# Strategic Recommendations

## 1. Strengthen Sneaker Visibility

Make Bata's existing youth-oriented sneaker portfolio easier to discover across:

- Website
- Social media
- Stores
- Youth-focused campaigns

---

## 2. Build Trend-Led Product Communication

Use:

- Outfit-based content
- Styling inspiration
- New product drops
- Short-form videos
- Contemporary product storytelling

instead of relying only on functional product messaging.

---

## 3. Student-Focused Value Strategy

Test:

- Student offers
- Campus promotions
- "Under ₹1,999" collections
- Targeted value bundles

---

## 4. Improve Youth Product Discoverability

Create clearer merchandising categories such as:

- Trending Sneakers
- Campus Picks
- Under ₹1,999
- Everyday Sneakers
- New Drops

---

## 5. Strengthen Campus & Social Engagement

Potential initiatives include:

- Campus ambassadors
- Student creators
- Micro-influencers
- User-generated content
- Short-form social campaigns

---

# Implementation Roadmap

# Final Conclusion

The project does not suggest that Bata needs to abandon its existing identity.

Instead, the analysis indicates an opportunity to connect Bata's existing strengths of:

**Affordability + Quality + Reliability**

with stronger:

**Style Relevance + Sneaker Visibility + Youth-Focused Communication**

The recommended approach is therefore to **extend Bata's existing value proposition into a more contemporary and visible youth proposition**, while validating the strategy through small-scale pilot testing before broader implementation.

---

# Study Limitations

This analysis is based on a **convenience sample of 14 respondents**.

Therefore:

- Findings are exploratory.
- Results should not be generalised to the entire Gen Z population in India.
- Some survey questions were not answered exactly according to the requested selection instructions.
- Recommendations should be validated through a larger and more diverse study before commercial implementation.
